The idea

Most furniture fails at a joint that was never meant to be opened.

We spent our first four years repairing other people’s furniture, which is an unusually good education. You learn that almost nothing fails because the wood gave up. It fails because a bolted apron worked loose over three hundred dinners, or a staple gun replaced a knot, or the whole thing was glued shut so the only repair available was a skip.

So we build the opposite. Every joint that carries weight can be re-wedged. Every cover comes off. The drawings for every piece we have shipped since 2014 are in a plan chest by the door, filed by the customer’s surname, which is how we can quote a repair on a fourteen-year-old sofa without asking you a single question.

— Iona Brackley & Fen Duthie, founders

Three species, no more

We only work in wood we know how to fix.

Air-dried ash

Perthshire coppice

Springy, pale, and forgiving under steam. Everything we bend is ash, and every bend is left to season a fortnight before it is fitted.

European oak

Jutland, single log runs

Heavy and slow-moving. We buy whole log runs so a shelf bay added in eight years still matches the four you own now.

Linden

Small mills, Southern Sweden

Stays pale for decades and takes a soap finish beautifully. Soft enough to dent, which we consider a feature of a bedroom.

From log to living room

Five steps,
one of which
takes years.

Lead times run eight to eighteen weeks. Almost none of that is machining; most of it is waiting for glue, oil, and steam-bent ash to make up their minds.

  1. 01

    The stack

    Timber comes in green and goes onto sticks in the yard. Twenty months minimum, usually more. We are working through boards bought in 2021.

  2. 02

    Cut list by hand

    Every board is chalked out by the maker who will build the piece, so the figure runs where a hand will land rather than where a spreadsheet put it.

  3. 03

    Joints, not fixings

    Mortise-and-tenon, drawbored and wedged. Screws appear in exactly two places across all four collections, and both are hanging rails.

  4. 04

    Finish, then wait

    Soap, hard wax oil, lye, or fumed. Then the piece sits in the workshop for four days so we can see it in four different lights before it leaves.

  5. 05

    Come back in ten years

    Re-strung webbing, re-waxed runners, rewoven paper cord. Repairs are quoted at bench rate, and we keep the drawings for every piece we have ever shipped.
