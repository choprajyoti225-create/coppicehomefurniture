Haldane

Seating that earns the dent it leaves in a rug.

Notes on the collection

Oldest piece still in service

14 yrs

Pieces in the collection

3

Haldane began as a favour. A retired shipwright in Fife asked us for a sofa he could nap on without folding himself in half — nothing tall, nothing tufted, and no legs he would trip over at four in the morning. We built one. He kept it fourteen years and sent it back for new webbing, which is the only compliment we take seriously.

The frame is mortise-and-tenon ash, air-dried for at least twenty months so the wood is done moving before we touch it. Suspension is jute webbing strung by hand across the seat rail — eleven passes on the three-seat, interlaced rather than stapled, so a single strand can be replaced without opening the upholstery. Cushions are latex core wrapped in wool batting: firm underneath, forgiving on top.

Nothing in Haldane is glued shut. Every joint that carries weight can be re-wedged, and every cover comes off with a slip knot and twelve minutes of patience.
