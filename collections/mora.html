02 · Dining rooms · 2021 — present

Mora

A table is a room with a top on it.

Notes on the collection

Fasteners in the base

None

Pieces in the collection

3

We drew Mora after a decade of repairing other people’s dining tables. Almost all of them failed in the same place: a bolted apron that racked loose after a few hundred meals, then split the top trying to hold on. So Mora has no bolts. The trestle is drawbored — the tenon holes offset by a millimetre and a half, so the oak pins pull the joint permanently tighter as they seat.

Tops are single-species boards, book-matched and jointed by hand, with a breadboard end that lets the width breathe through a Scottish winter. We leave the underside unfinished on purpose. Run a hand beneath the overhang and you can feel the plane tracks; that is where the piece tells you it was made by somebody.

The chairs came second, and only because customers kept asking what to put around it. They are steam-bent, stacked three high in the workshop, and light enough for a child to carry to the table.
